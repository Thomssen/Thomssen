What is inside

One search bar. Seven tools.

Investigate

Type an IP address, domain, email address, username, GitHub account, file hash or URL, or press Ctrl+K from anywhere. Thomsen OSINT works out what it is and runs every lookup that applies. Each step ends as confirmed, not found, inconclusive or skipped, and inconclusive is never shown as clean. Suggested next lookups are one click away, and batch mode runs a whole list.

Username

Check 78 sites automatically for a username, plus name variations such as johndoe, john_doe and jdoe that you pick one by one. Thirteen more networks that can't be checked reliably, such as Instagram, X and TikTok, are offered as links to open by hand. A page that blocks automated checks is shown as unknown, never as a found profile.

Domain

Look up public information about a domain, including its DNS records, registrar and expiry dates, and TLS certificate. Subdomains come from public certificate-transparency logs.

IP

Look up public information about an IP address, such as its network, provider and approximate country and region. It never tries to find a precise physical location.

Email

Check an email address against public sources, such as its format, mail setup and Gravatar. Add your own Have I Been Pwned key to also check known data breaches.

GitHub

Look up a GitHub profile or organization and search public repositories by keyword. It uses GitHub's public API, so no account or token is needed to get started.

Metadata

Read the metadata stored in images, PDFs and Office documents, such as EXIF data and document properties. Files are only read on your PC, never uploaded or changed.

Hash Tools

Calculate MD5, SHA-1, SHA-256 and SHA-512 hashes for any text, and fingerprint a local file.

Built for real casework

More than a lookup tool.

Watchlist

Watch a domain, IP address, email address or username. It is checked hourly, every 6 or 12 hours, daily or weekly and compared with the first check. You get a Windows notification when something changes, such as a new DNS record, a changed certificate, a new open port or a new profile. It can keep running from the system tray and start with Windows.

Evidence mode

Switch it on for a case and every lookup, note, target and exported report is fingerprinted with SHA-256, time-stamped, chained to the one before it and sealed with a signature only your PC can make. Results cannot be deleted or edited while it is on. One click verifies the whole record and names anything that was changed, removed or slipped in. Export an evidence package and anyone can re-check it without the app.

Cases

Keep related findings together. A case holds your targets, results and notes on your PC, with tags, a dated journal, starred results and a timeline of everything that happened. Compare shows what changed between two results, you can search across every case, and cases export and import as files. Every tool also works without one.

Relationship graph

See how the domains, subdomains, IP addresses, name and mail servers, ASNs, organizations, email addresses, usernames and profiles in a case connect. Solid lines are what a source reported, dashed lines are the app's own deduction, and something mentioned by two results becomes one node. Drag, zoom, search and filter.

Reports

Export the open investigation as PDF, HTML, Markdown, CSV, JSON or Text. A report has a cover, a summary, targets, journal, findings with their status and sources, a timeline and a plain-language guide to reading it. CSV takes a comma or semicolon separator so it opens in columns in Excel in your region. PDFs are printed on your PC and nothing is uploaded.

Opsec

Send lookups through a proxy or Tor, add a random delay between requests, or switch on passive-only mode so nothing is sent to the investigated target itself, only to third-party databases. If the proxy is down the lookup fails; it never falls back to a direct connection.

Updates

Thomsen OSINT checks for new versions and installs them after one click. Updates are signed, and the app only accepts installers made by Thomsen.

Good to know

What it does and does not do.

Evidence mode

It proves the record has not changed since it was made, and which PC made it. It does not prove a source told the truth, and timestamps come from your PC's clock.

Proxy, Tor and passive-only

They reduce what the target sees. They do not make anyone anonymous, and third-party sources still see the question. Tor mode needs Tor running on your PC.

PDF reports

Printing a PDF needs Microsoft Edge or Google Chrome installed on your PC.

Watchlist

The Windows watchlist and notifications only run while your subscription is active.

Local first

Cases are stored on your PC and never uploaded. The app contacts the license server to check your subscription and look for updates, and the public sources you ask it about.

Your subscription

€10 a month, one PC at a time. If it ends, your investigations stay on your PC and the app locks until you subscribe again.

FAQ

Questions about Thomsen OSINT.

Does Thomsen OSINT upload my investigations?

No. Cases, notes and reports are stored on your PC. The app contacts the public sources you ask it about, and the license server to check your subscription and look for updates.

Do I need API keys?

No, most features work without one. VirusTotal and AbuseIPDB need your own key (add it under Settings → API Keys). Keys for urlscan.io, GitHub and ipinfo.io are optional and raise limits.

What is evidence mode?

A tamper-evident record of a case: every result is fingerprinted, time-stamped and sealed, so any later change is found. It proves the record has not changed, not that a source was right.

Can I use it with Tor or a proxy?

Yes (Settings → Opsec). There is also a passive-only mode that never contacts the target itself.

How do updates work?

The app checks in the background and installs a new version after one click. Updates are signed. Versions older than 1.2.0 are no longer supported and must be updated.

What happens to my cases if I cancel?

They stay on your PC. The app locks until you subscribe again.
